Over lood

Overload in programming refers to a feature that allows multiple methods, constructors, or operators
to have the same name but behave differently based on their input parameters.
It is a form of polymorphism, enabling developers to define different behaviors for similar operations in a more readable and organized way.

1. Electrical Overload:
In electrical systems, an overload occurs when the current flowing through a circuit exceeds its designed capacity.
This can lead to overheating and potentially cause damage to equipment or even fires.
Protective devices like fuses or circuit breakers are used to prevent such occurrences.

2. Workload Overload:
In a professional or academic setting, overload refers to having too much work or responsibility, leading to stress or decreased performance.
Managing priorities and delegating tasks can help alleviate this.

3. Programming (Method Overloading):
In computer science, method overloading is a feature in object-oriented programming where two or more methods in the same class have the same name but different parameters.
This allows flexibility in how methods are used based on the input type or number of arguments.

4. Mechanical Overload:
In machinery, overload happens when a machine is subjected to forces beyond its design limits, leading to wear, malfunction, or failure.

5. Information Overload:
This refers to being overwhelmed by excessive information, making it difficult to process or make decisions.
It is a common challenge in the digital age.
