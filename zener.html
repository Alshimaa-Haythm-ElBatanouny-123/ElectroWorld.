zener



A Zener diode is a special type of diode designed to operate in the reverse breakdown region to regulate voltage.
Unlike regular diodes, which are damaged if the reverse voltage exceeds their breakdown voltage.
A zener diode is designed to conduct electricity in reverse once a specific voltage, called the Zener voltage (Vz), is reached.

Key Features of a Zener Diode

1. Voltage Regulation:
Maintains a stable output voltage across its terminals when reverse-biased and operating in the breakdown region.

2. Directional Behavior:
Allows current to flow in the forward direction like a standard diode, but also conducts in reverse after reaching the Zener voltage.

3. Zener Voltage:
A precisely defined breakdown voltage (e.g., 5.1V, 12V) set during manufacturing.

Applications of Zener Diodes

1. Voltage Regulation:
Used in power supplies to stabilize output voltage.

2. Overvoltage Protection:
Protects sensitive components by clamping voltage to a safe level.

3. Reference Voltage:
Provides a stable reference voltage in circuits like analog-to-digital converters.

In operation, a Zener diode is typically connected in parallel with the load to maintain a
constant voltage despite variations in input voltage or load conditions.
