Gas Sensor



A gas sensor, also known as a gas detector, is an electronic device that detects the presence and concentration of gases in the environment.
It is widely used for safety, environmental monitoring, and industrial applications.

Working Principle

Gas sensors work by detecting changes in the air when a specific gas is present.
Most common gas sensors use semiconducting materials like metal-oxide semiconductors (MOS).
These materials change their electrical resistance when exposed to a target gas.

For example:
* In clean air, the sensor has a certain resistance.
* When a gas like methane, carbon monoxide, or smoke is present, the resistance changes.
* This change is converted into an electrical signal, which can be read by a microcontroller or system.

Popular Types of Gas Sensors

1. MQ Series Sensors – The most commonly used gas sensors for Arduino and Raspberry Pi projects.
* MQ-2: Detects LPG, smoke, methane, propane, hydrogen.
* MQ-3: Detects alcohol, ethanol.
* MQ-7: Detects carbon monoxide (CO).
* MQ-135: Detects air quality gases like CO2, NH3, benzene.

2. Electrochemical Gas Sensors

* Detect gases using chemical reactions that produce a small electric current.
* Highly accurate, often used in professional safety equipment.

3. Infrared (IR) Gas Sensors

* Detect gases based on how gas molecules absorb infrared light.
* Mostly used for CO2 and hydrocarbon detection.

How MQ-2 Gas Sensor Works

* 1. The sensor has a heating element and a metal oxide sensing layer.
* 2. When gas is present, it reacts with the heated surface, changing the resistance.
* 3. The sensor outputs an analog voltage corresponding to the gas concentration.
* 4. A microcontroller (like Arduino) reads this voltage to determine gas levels.

Applications

* Home gas leak detection
* Smoke alarms
* Industrial gas monitoring
* Air quality measurement
* Automotive safety systems

Important Specifications

* Detection range (e.g., 200–10000 ppm)
* Response time (e.g., 10 seconds)
* Heater voltage (usually 5V)
* Sensitivity to specific gases
