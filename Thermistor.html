Thermistor


A Thermistor (short for Thermal Resistor) is a type of resistor whose electrical resistance changes significantly with temperature.
It is commonly used to measure or regulate temperature in various applications.
Thermistors rely on the principle of thermal resistance, where the resistance varies depending on the surrounding temperature.

Types of Thermistors>

There are two main types of thermistors based on how their resistance changes with temperature:

1. Positive Temperature Coefficient (PTC):
* Resistance increases as the temperature rises.
* Often used for circuit protection, such as preventing overcurrent.

2. Negative Temperature Coefficient (NTC):
* Resistance decreases as the temperature rises.
* Commonly used in temperature sensing and control applications.

Key Features of Thermistors

1. High Sensitivity to Temperature:
Their resistance changes significantly with small temperature variations,
making them more precise than many other temperature sensors.

2. Limited Temperature Range:
Typically designed to operate within specific temperature ranges.

3. Compact and Fast-Responding:
They are small in size and react quickly to temperature changes.

Applications of Thermistors

1. Temperature Measurement:
Used in digital thermometers, air conditioning systems, and refrigerators.

2. Temperature Control:
Found in heating systems, battery management systems, and thermostats.

3. Circuit Protection:
PTC thermistors can protect circuits by limiting current during faults.

In summary

thermistors are versatile, cost-effective components widely used in both industrial and everyday temperature monitoring and control systems.
