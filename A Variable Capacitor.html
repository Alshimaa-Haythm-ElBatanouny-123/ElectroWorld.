Variable Capacitor


A Variable Capacitor is a type of capacitor whose capacitance value can be adjusted or varied manually or electronically.
Unlike fixed capacitors, the capacitance of a variable capacitor is not constant and can be changed as needed for specific applications.

Structure and Working

It typically consists of two sets of metal plates:

Stationary Plates:
Fixed in place.

Movable Plates:
Can rotate or slide to change the overlap area with the stationary plates.

* The dielectric material (such as air, ceramic, or plastic) separates the plates.
* Changing the overlap area or the distance between the plates alters the capacitance.

Applications

Tuning Circuits:
Used in radios, TVs, and other communication devices to select specific frequencies.

Oscillators:
Found in frequency-generating circuits.

Antenna Matching:
Adjusting the impedance of antennas for better signal transmission.

Advantages

* Provides flexibility in circuit design.
* Can be manually controlled for precision tuning.

Disadvantages

* Limited capacitance range compared to fixed capacitors.
* Mechanically delicate and prone to wear over time.
