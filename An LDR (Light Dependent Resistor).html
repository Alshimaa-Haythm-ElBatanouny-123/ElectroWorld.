An LDR (Light Dependent Resistor)


An LDR (Light Dependent Resistor), also known as a photoresistor,
is a type of resistor whose resistance changes based on the amount of light falling on its surface.
It is a simple and cost-effective sensor used to detect light intensity.

Structure

* Made of a semiconductor material (such as cadmium sulfide, CdS).

* In darkness or low light, the resistance of the LDR is very high (in the megaohms range).

* When exposed to bright light, its resistance drops significantly (to a few hundred ohms).

Working Principle

* The semiconductor material's conductivity increases as photons from light strike it.
* This reduces the resistance and allows more current to flow through the LDR.

Characteristics

* Non-linear response: The change in resistance is not directly proportional to the light intensity.

* Slow response time: Takes time to adjust to sudden changes in light.

Applications

1. Light Sensors:
Used in automatic streetlights, night lights, and garden lights.

2. Photographic Exposure Meters:
Detects light levels for proper camera exposure settings.

3. Security Systems:
For detecting the presence or absence of light (e.g., in alarm systems).

4. Brightness Control:
Used in display screens for automatic brightness adjustment.

Advantages

* Simple and low-cost.

* Easy to integrate into electronic circuits.

Disadvantages

* Affected by environmental factors like temperature and humidity.

* Slower response compared to advanced light sensors like photodiodes or phototransistors.
