Relays



A relay is an electromechanical or electronic device used to control the flow of electricity in a circuit.
It operates as a switch, allowing a low-power signal to control a high-power circuit without direct physical contact. Here's a breakdown of how it works.

1. Basic Components:

Coil:
A wire wound around a core that creates a magnetic field when current flows through it.

Armature:
A movable lever or switch that reacts to the magnetic field generated by the coil.

Contacts:
Metallic terminals that open or close to allow or stop the flow of electricity.

2. How It Works:



When a small electric current flows through the coil, it generates a magnetic field.
This magnetic field pulls the armature, causing the contacts to either open (disconnect) or close (connect) the circuit.
When the current to the coil stops, the magnetic field disappears, and a spring mechanism returns the armature to its original position.

3. Types of Relays:

Uses physical moving parts, like the armature and spring, to operate.
Solid-State Relay (SSR): Uses electronic components (e.g., transistors) to switch circuits, with no moving parts.

4. Applications:

Relays are used in various applications.such as:


Automobiles:
To control lights, horns, and starters.

Home Appliances:
For controlling motors, heaters, and compressors.

Home Appliances:
For switching large currents in machinery or control panels.
