Avometer



A multimeter, also known as an avometer (short for Amperes, Volts, and Ohms meter),
is an electronic measuring instrument that combines several measurement functions in one unit.
It is commonly used to test electrical circuits and components.

1. Measuring Voltage (V):

Measures electrical potential difference between two points.

Two types:
DC Voltage (V— or DCV):
Used for batteries, DC power supplies.

AC Voltage (V~ or ACV):
Used for household electricity.

2. Measuring Current (A):

Measures the flow of electric current.

Two types:
* DC Current (A— or DCA)
* AC Current (A~ or ACA)

Must be connected in series with the circuit.

3. Measuring Resistance (Ω):

* Checks the resistance of a component or circuit.
* Useful for testing resistors or checking continuity.

4. Continuity Test:

* Checks if a circuit is closed (i.e., current can flow).
* Often uses a beeping sound when continuity is detected.

5. Diode Test:

* Tests diodes to see if they conduct in one direction.

6. Additional Features (in some multimeters):

* Capacitance measurement
* Frequency measurement
* Temperature measurement
* Transistor testing

Types of Multimeters

1. Analog Multimeter:
* Uses a needle to display readings.
* More sensitive to voltage changes.
* Less common today.

2. Digital Multimeter (DMM):
* Uses a digital display (LCD).
* More accurate and easier to read.
* Widely used in modern electronics.

Main Parts of a Digital Multimeter

Display Screen: Shows the measured value.

Rotary Switch (Dial):
Selects the function (voltage, current, resistance, etc.)

Probes (Leads):
Black and red wires used to connect to the circuit.

Black lead:
COM (common ground)

Red lead:
VΩmA or 10A input (depending on the measurement)

Ports (Sockets):
* COM port (for black probe)
* VΩmA port (for voltage, resistance, and small currents)
* 10A or 20A port (for measuring high currents)

Safety Tips

* Always start with the highest range if you’re not sure of the value.
* Never measure current in parallel.
* Use the correct port for the type of measurement.
* Be cautious when measuring high voltages.
