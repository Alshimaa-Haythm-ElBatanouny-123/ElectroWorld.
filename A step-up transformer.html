Step-up transformer



A step-up transformer is a type of electrical transformer designed to increase the voltage from a lower value
on the primary winding to a higher value on the secondary winding.
while decreasing the current proportionally to maintain constant power (in ideal conditions).

Working Principle

A step-up transformer operates based on Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction
When alternating current (AC) flows through the primary winding, it creates a varying magnetic field.
This magnetic field induces a voltage in the secondary winding.
If the secondary winding has more turns than the primary winding, the output voltage will be higher.

Construction



1. Primary Winding
Fewer turns of wire.
Connected to the low-voltage source.

2. Secondary Winding
More turns of wire.
Produces a higher output voltage.

3. Core
Made of laminated iron or ferrite to efficiently transfer the magnetic flux between the windings.

Voltage Ratio

The amount by which the voltage is increased depends on the turns ratio of the transformer

V2/V1 = N2N1

V1:Primary voltage.
V2:Secondary volt
N1:Number of turns in the primary winding.
V2:Number of turns in the secondary winding.

Applications

1. Power Transmission
Used in power stations to step up voltage to very high levels (e.g., 132 kV or more) for efficient transmission over long distances, minimizing energy loss.

2. Electrical Devices
Found in devices like ignition systems, microwave ovens, and X-ray machines to generate high voltages.

3. Renewable Energy Systems
Converts low voltage from wind turbines or solar panels to usable grid-level voltages.

Advantages

* Reduces power losses in long-distance transmission.
* Provides electrical isolation between circuits.
* Step-up transformers are crucial in modern power systems for efficient energy distribution and specialized applications.
