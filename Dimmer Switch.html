Dimmer Switch




A dimmer switch is an electrical switch used to adjust the brightness of light fixtures,
allowing users to control the intensity of the light in a room.
Unlike regular on/off switches, dimmer switches provide a variable range of light output,
giving flexibility in creating different moods and saving energy.

Features

Adjustable Light Intensity:
The primary function of a dimmer switch is to control the brightness of a light.
It can reduce the light output to a lower level or increase it to full brightness.

Energy Saving:
By dimming the lights, you use less electricity, which can result in energy savings,
especially for incandescent bulbs.

Creates Ambiance:
Dimmers are often used in living rooms, dining rooms, bedrooms,
] or theaters to create a comfortable or cinematic ambiance by adjusting light levels.

Types:
There are various types of dimmer switches, including rotary (knob-type),
slider (sliding bar), and touch-sensitive dimmers.

How It Works

Dimmer switches work by controlling the amount of electrical current that reaches the light bulb.
In older dimmer designs, this was achieved by using a resistive control,
while modern dimmers typically use a technology called phase-cut dimming (either leading-edge or trailing-edge).
which adjusts the waveform of the AC electricity to reduce the amount of power delivered to the bulb.

This makes dimmer switches ideal for applications where lighting flexibility is needed, such as in home theaters,
restaurants, and living spaces. They are available for various types of light bulbs
including incandescent, halogen, CFL, and LED lights, with newer models specifically designed for energy-efficient bulbs.
