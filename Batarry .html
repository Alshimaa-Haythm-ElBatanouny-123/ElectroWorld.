Batteries



Batteries are devices that store and provide electrical energy through chemical reactions.
They consist of one or more electrochemical cells, each containing two electrodes: a positive terminal (cathode) and a negative terminal (anode), separated by an electrolyte.
When a battery is connected to a device, a chemical reaction occurs inside the cell,
causing electrons to flow from the anode to the cathode, generating an electric current.

There are various types of batteries, including:

1. Primary Batteries:
* These are single-use batteries, such as alkaline or lithium batteries, which cannot be recharged once depleted.

2. Secondary Batteries:
* These are rechargeable batteries, such as lead-acid, nickel-cadmium (NiCd), and lithium-ion (Li-ion) batteries.
* They can be recharged multiple times by reversing the chemical reaction.
Batteries are used in many applications, including powering electronic devices, vehicles, and renewable energy systems.
The energy they store depends on the materials used in the electrodes and electrolyte, and battery
capacity is measured in ampere-hours (Ah) or milliampere-hours (mAh), indicating how much charge the battery can hold.
