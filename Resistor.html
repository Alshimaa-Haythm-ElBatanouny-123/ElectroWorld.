Resistor


A resistor is a fundamental electronic component used in circuits to limit or regulate the flow of electrical current.
It resists the flow of electrons, which causes a drop in voltage across its terminals.
Resistors are measured in ohms (O), which is the unit of electrical resistance.

How a Resistor Works

A resistor works by converting electrical energy into heat, which reduces the current flow in the circuit.

It obeys Ohm's Law


V = I \ R

* Voltage across the resistor (in volts)
* Current through the resistor (in amperes)
* Resistance (in ohms)

Types of Resistors:


1. Fixed Resistors:
Have a constant resistance value.

Examples: Carbon film resistors, metal film resistors.

2. Variable Resistors:
Allow the resistance to be adjusted.

Examples: Potentiometers, rheostats.

3. Special Resistors:
Designed for specific purposes, such as thermistors (temperature-dependent) and light-dependent resistors (LDRs).

Applications of Resistors:


1. Current Limiting: Prevents excessive current that could damage components.
2. Voltage Division: Creates a specific voltage level in circuits.
3. Signal Conditioning: Adjusts or filters signals in electronic devices.
4. Heat Generation: In appliances like electric heaters.
