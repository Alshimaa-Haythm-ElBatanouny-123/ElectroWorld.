Step-down transformer



A step-down transformer (also known as a voltage reducer) is an electrical device used to decrease the voltage from a higher level to a lower level.
It operates on the principle of electromagnetic induction.
The transformer consists of two coils of wire: the primary coil, which receives the high voltage, and the secondary coil, which delivers the reduced voltage.

When alternating current (AC) passes through the primary coil, it creates a magnetic field that induces a voltage in the secondary coil.
The ratio of the number of turns of wire in the primary coil to the number of turns in the secondary coil determines the voltage reduction.
If the secondary coil has fewer turns than the primary coil, the voltage is reduced.

For example, in a step-down transformer, if the primary coil has 1000 turns and the secondary coil has 200 turns, the voltage will be reduced by a factor of 5.

These transformers are commonly used in power distribution systems, such as in electrical substations.
To step down high transmission voltages to levels suitable for residential and commercial use.
