N-Channel MOSFET



An N-Channel MOSFET (Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor) is a type of transistor used for switching and amplification.
It belongs to the family of FETs and operates by controlling the flow of current between the drain and source terminals
using a voltage applied to the gate terminal.
N-channel MOSFETs are widely used due to their efficiency and fast switching capabilities.

Structure of an N-Channel MOSFET

1. Three Terminals:
Gate (G):
Controls the transistor's operation by creating an electric field.
Source (S):
Electrons flow out through this terminal.
Drain (D):
Electrons flow in through this terminal.

2. Channel Type:
The channel is made of N-type material, which allows electrons (majority carriers) to flow from the drain to the source.

3. Types of N-Channel MOSFETs:
Enhancement Mode:
The transistor is OFF by default and requires a positive gate voltage to turn ON.
Depletion Mode:
The transistor is ON by default and requires a negative gate voltage to turn OFF.

Working Principle

An N-channel MOSFET operates by modulating the electric field in the channel region between the drain and source.

1. Enhancement Mode:
When the gate-source voltage () is zero or negative, the MOSFET is OFF, and no current flows between the drain and source.
When is positive and exceeds a threshold voltage (), an electric field forms, creating a conductive channel for electrons.
This allows current () to flow from drain to source.

2. Depletion Mode:
When is zero, the MOSFET is ON, and current flows through the pre-existing channel.
Applying a negative depletes the channel, turning the MOSFET OFF.

Key Characteristics

1. High Input Impedance:
The gate is insulated by an oxide layer, requiring very little current to control the MOSFET.

2. Voltage-Controlled Device:
Operated by gate voltage rather than current, unlike BJTs.

3. Switching Speed:
Very fast switching due to low gate capacitance.

3. Switching Speed:

Applications

Used in power supplies, motor controllers, and DC-DC converters.

P-Channel MOSFET



A P-Channel MOSFET (Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor) is a type of transistor used for switching and amplification.
It works by controlling the flow of current between the source and drain terminals using a voltage applied to the gate terminal.
Unlike an N-Channel MOSFET, the P-Channel MOSFET uses holes (positive charge carriers) as the majority carriers.

Structure of a P-Channel MOSFET

1. Three Terminals
Gate (G):
Controls the flow of current between the source and drain.
Source (S):
The terminal from which current enters.
Drain (D):
The terminal where current exits.

2. Channel Type:
The channel is made of P-type material, which conducts when a negative voltage is applied to the gate relative to the source.

3. Types of P-Channel MOSFETs:
Enhancement Mode:
The MOSFET is OFF by default and requires a negative gate voltage to turn ON.
Depletion Mode:
The MOSFET is ON by default and requires a positive gate voltage to turn OFF.

2. Depletion Mode:

Applying a positive depletes the channel, turning the MOSFET OFF.

Key Characteristics

1. High Input Impedance:
The gate is insulated by an oxide layer, requiring minimal current to control the MOSFET.

2. Voltage-Controlled Device:
Operated by gate voltage rather than current, similar to an N-Channel MOSFET.

3. Polarity:
Source and drain are reversed compared to N-Channel MOSFETs, with current flowing from source to drain.

4. On-Resistance ():
Resistance between the drain and source when the MOSFET is ON. Typically higher than in N-Channel MOSFETs.

Applications

1. High-Side Switching:
Used to connect loads to a positive voltage supply.

2. Amplifiers:

3. Power Management:
Used in battery chargers, DC-DC converters, and power supply circuits.

4. Complementary Logic Circuits:
Works in combination with N-Channel MOSFETs in CMOS technology.

Comparison with N-Channel MOSFET

P-Channel MOSFET:
Current flows from source to drain.
Turns ON with a negative gate voltage.

N-Channel MOSFET: Current flows from drain to source.
Turns ON with a positive gate voltage.

The P-Channel MOSFET is an essential component in circuits that require high-side switching or polarity-sensitive applications.
It complements the N-Channel MOSFET in many designs,
especially in CMOS and power management systems.
