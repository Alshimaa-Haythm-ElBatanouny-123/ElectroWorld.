PIR (Passive Infrared) Sensors



A Passive Infrared (PIR) sensor is an electronic device
That detects motion by measuring infrared (IR) radiation emitted by objects in its field of view. It is called "passive" because it does not emit any energy; instead.
it detects infrared radiation naturally emitted by warm objects, such as humans and animals.

How Does a PIR Sensor Work

1. Infrared Detection:
All objects with a temperature above absolute zero emit infrared radiation.
Humans and animals, being warm-blooded, emit a significant amount of IR radiation.

2. Sensing Mechanism:
The PIR sensor consists of a pyroelectric sensor that detects changes in infrared radiation levels.
It is usually covered with a Fresnel lens, which focuses IR radiation onto the sensor.

3. Motion Detection:
The sensor has two slots that measure IR radiation.
When an object moves in front of the sensor, the change in radiation levels between the two slots generates an electrical signal.
which is then processed to detect motion.

Key Features of PIR Sensors

Energy Efficient:
Consumes very little power, making it ideal for battery-powered applications.

Wide Detection Range:
Typically covers an angle of 90° to 180°, with a range of 3 to 10 meters.

Reliable and Cost-Effective:
Commonly used in security systems and automation.

Passive Operation:
Does not emit waves, reducing interference with other devices.

Applications of PIR Sensors

Security Systems:
Used in motion-activated alarms and surveillance cameras.

Lighting Automation:
Controls lights in homes and offices based on movement.

Smart Homes:
Integrated into IoT systems for energy-saving and automation.

Robotics:
Helps robots detect human presence and navigate environments.

Advantages of PIR Sensors

* Low power consumption
* Simple and easy to integrate
* Works well in various environments
* Does not require a direct line of sight

Limitations of PIR Sensors

* Cannot detect stationary objects
* Affected by temperature fluctuations
* Limited range compared to other motion sensors like microwave sensors.
